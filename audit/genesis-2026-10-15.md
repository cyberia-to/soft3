### what blocks the bostrom root

Not row 43. The builder loads every table into one in-memory `BbgState` and
would commit once; the per-batch recommit measured on 2026-09-27
(`cybergraph/audit/import-commit-cost-2026-09-27.md`) is never entered, and
the load itself is a minute.

The block is the commitment size cap: `lens/brakedown/src/lib.rs`
`MAX_VARIABLES = 20` — one dimension commits as a multilinear polynomial of at
most 2^20 field elements, and `commit_fields` panics past it
(`Brakedown variable limit`, hit on the first run before the guard existed).
bostrom needs:

| dimension | fields | needs |
|---|---|---|
| particles | 85,212,823 | 2^27 |
| files | 31,436,503 | 2^25 |
| balances | 28,803,918 | 2^25 |
| axons_in | 27,298,057 | 2^25 |
| axons_out | 26,667,310 | 2^25 |
| signals | 10,256,293 | 2^24 |
| cards | 765,395 | fits |
| neurons | 678,428 | fits |

Particles is 81× over the cap. Two of those dimensions are inflated by engine
semantics worth revisiting before the cap is lifted: `BbgState::insert`
creates one zero balance per link endpoint (2,949,732 links → 3,200,435
balance rows, of which 56,785 are real), and every link also becomes an
axon-particle in `particles` (6,086,630 entries for 3,143,650 content
particles). Even with those gone, particles needs 2^26.

Beyond the cap, a 2^27 Brakedown commit has not been timed; the pussy root
(2^20 dimensions) takes under 5 s in total, so a linear extrapolation puts a
single bostrom commit in minutes, not days — the question is bbg/lens's
limit and memory, and that is launch row 43's second half (incremental
commitments), not this pipeline.
