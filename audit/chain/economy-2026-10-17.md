## findings for the owner and the spec

1. **The new-high rule does not bound a quiet-epoch recycler.** Attribution
   scales `x(c)` by `N/R`, but `pay = min(cap, ⌊B · x / X⌋)` normalises by
   `X`: a relock that is the only earner of its epoch takes `min(cap, B)`
   however small its `x`. The blessed new-high scenario shows it: the
   first lock was paid 649,257, the relock under a mark two decays below the old
   height (99.6 % of it) 1,785,458. The bound left is the 1 % cap per settlement and the
   7-day lock per cycle, not "the mark's decay" of §9. A fix that keeps
   everything else: cap on the new height, `cap(c) = ⌊Δs · (N/R) ·
   R_MAX_PPM / 10^6⌋` (or scale the budget by `Σ x / Σ a⁺`). Not
   implemented — a spec decision.
2. **Under J = D_KL(φ ‖ u), most new links lower syntropy.** Stake on a
   minor edge spreads focus (∂J/∂A < 0) and earns nothing as +1; it earns
   as −1. On the test graphs the earners are +1 on dominant edges and −1
   against minor ones. This is the spec's objective working as written;
   the owner should see it before launch (rewards.md §1 "pay for descent").
3. **The record's `syntropy: i64` overflows on bostrom.** J(φ*_0) is
   9,886,742,015,786,730,065 at 2^60 (8.58 nats) > i64::MAX; the kernel
   saturates. tru's agent reports an amendment to i128 is coming; the record
   grows to 230 bytes then.
4. **Hedging pays.** +1 and −1 of equal size on one axon leave A unchanged
   and the +1 side is paid as if it had added weight (§9 says "capital
   doubled for the same credit" — that is the behaviour; the credit is for
   no change of the graph).
